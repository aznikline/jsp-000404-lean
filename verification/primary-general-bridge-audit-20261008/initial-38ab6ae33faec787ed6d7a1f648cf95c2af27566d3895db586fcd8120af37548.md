## Definitions and scope

A generalized configuration (GC) is a hierarchy of disjoint circles;
every nonprimitive circle has at least two children. For points p and q,
their generalized direction GD(pq) is the direction between the centers
of the first separate containing circles. GA(V) is the maximum
generalized angle. A perfect nonprimitive circle has exactly two children
with equivalent contents; a perfect GC has this property at every
nonprimitive circle. A primitive range-one circle, containing one point,
is allowed: its exponent is zero. Perfect does not mean saturated.

Let Q={o_1,...,o_s} be the distinct range-one centers. At o_i, the full
cyclic gaps between the projective lines o_i o_j are g_ij=pi*phi_ij,
with sum g_ij=pi. Put

\[
\lambda=2\pi/u,\quad n=\lfloor\pi/\lambda\rfloor,
\quad\delta=\pi/\lambda-n,\quad
\kappa_i=\sum_j(\lfloor g_{ij}/\lambda\rfloor-1)_+.
\]

Lemma 3.4 assigns weight 2^k to a perfect range-one circle with k distinct
internal generalized line directions. Lemmas 3.7–3.8 maximize this local
content to 2^kappa_i. Thus the project quantity corresponding to the
paper's completed perfectized cardinality is
Phi=sum_i 2^kappa_i, using the **original actual rows**, rather than only
positive gaps or a chosen phase.

The LOW strip is 0<=delta<1/2, equivalently
pi/(n+1/2)<lambda<=pi/n. The HIGH strip is 1/2<=delta<1,
equivalently pi/(n+1)<lambda<=pi/(n+1/2).
The requested capacities are respectively 2^n and 2^n+2^(n-2).
The paper commonly writes GA(V)<=pi-lambda; original
`Section4Legality` uses strict actual angle bounds. These endpoint
conditions must be retained in any formal import of the argument.

## Key lemma 1: what the sector argument actually proves

**Lemma 3.3, printed p. 34.** Let V be a GC, Q any subset of its points,
and T_1,...,T_k disjoint double sectors of aperture Lambda. Every
generalized direction of **every pair of distinct points of Q** must be
inside one of this single common family of sectors. If
GA(V)<=pi-Lambda, then |Q|<=2^k.

Here a double sector is a projective direction interval together with its
opposite component; its two components distinguish the two orientations
of a line. The following expands the paper's graph argument without
using the desired cardinality bound as an assumption:

1. Color the edge {p,q} of the complete graph on Q by its sector. Because
   the sectors are disjoint and every pair is covered, this is an edge
   partition into k graphs G_j.
2. Suppose one G_j contains an odd cycle p_0,...,p_(2r),p_0. Orient its
   edges along the cycle and record which of T_j's two components each
   vector GD(p_t p_(t+1)) occupies. An odd circular binary sequence
   cannot alternate at every step. Two consecutive directed edges
   therefore lie in the same component.
3. Their directions differ by strictly less than Lambda when inside the
   component. The generalized angle at their common endpoint uses the
   negative of the incoming direction, so it exceeds pi-Lambda. This is
   Lemma 3.2 and contradicts the GA bound. Hence every G_j has no odd
   cycle and is bipartite.
4. Choose a bipartition bit for each G_j, including isolated vertices
   arbitrarily. Give each p its k-bit vector. Two distinct vertices cannot
   have the same vector: their complete-graph edge belongs to some G_j,
   whose bit separates its endpoints. The vectors are injective, yielding
   |Q|<=2^k. This is the content of Lemma 3.1 used by 3.3.

The word **inside** is material in the paper's short strict-angle
contradiction. If an implementation uses closed sectors, the elementary
angle estimate is only >=pi-Lambda. Original strict legality still
contradicts this; for the paper's non-strict GA bound, endpoint handling
must be supplied rather than silently replacing inside by membership in
a closed interval. This audit does not certify an unproved closed-sector
variant of the paper lemma.

This is one global edge coloring of one point set. Applying it separately
to rows or clumps does not give a combined coloring, and adding those
separate powers does not prove the desired total capacity. In particular,
Lemma 3.3 does not assert that a family of n suitable sectors exists for
an arbitrary legal center configuration. Introducing that existence as a
caller premise would leave the crucial geometry unproved.

## Key lemma 2: local gap capacity and what perfectization preserves

**Lemma 3.7, printed p. 36, with Lemma 3.8's immediate conclusion.**
Fix the original GC, its s range-one centers, and one circle c_i with
N_i internal points. Its gap capacity is exactly kappa_i above. The lemma
concludes N_i<=2^kappa_i. It then constructs perfect local contents and
concludes that a perfect GC V* exists with the **same range-one centers**,
at least as many points, and the same maximum generalized angle.

The complete dependency is local:

1. Lemma 3.5 divides every triple into: three different range-one
   circles; two points in one circle and one in another; or all three in
   one circle. Its formula is
   GA(V)=max(A(Q), max_i GA(P_i), pi-min_i beta_i), where beta_i is the
   least projective angle between an internal generalized line at o_i
   and an external line o_i o_j.
2. Consequently every internal generalized line at o_i is separated by
   at least lambda from every external line. In a full external gap of
   width g, it lies between the two lambda margins. Gaps below 2lambda
   contribute no interior allowed direction.
3. Write h=floor(g/lambda). The paper places h-1 double sectors of width
   lambda at lambda offsets in this gap (formula 3.11), when this number
   is positive. The allowed interval has width g-2lambda<(h-1)lambda,
   so this number of sectors suffices. For strict actual legality the
   margins are open; finite sector boundaries can be shifted to avoid
   the finitely many internal directions. The exact g=2lambda and
   non-strict endpoints require explicit treatment; the paper itself
   mentions g=2lambda in selecting the gaps. They are not an extra
   positive-gap score term.
4. Across all full gaps this produces kappa_i sectors covering all
   internal pair directions of **P_i**. Lemma 3.3 applied to Q=P_i gives
   N_i<=2^kappa_i. It is not applied here to the union of all P_i.
5. Lemma 3.6 supplies a binary hierarchy with 2^kappa_i points from
   lambda-separated internal lines. Its scaled copies retain those
   line directions. Filling each range-one circle this way preserves
   the centers, their external directions and their gap capacities;
   Lemma 3.5 checks the generalized-angle bound. This is the
   perfectization used in Lemma 3.8.

Thus these lemmas justify the **local weight formula** and replacement of
local contents by powers of two. They do not delete or merge a center,
make a new original score n-1, or impose an ordering relation on different
centers' kappa_i. A same-scale conversion from a non-strict GC bound to a
strict ordinary legal configuration also needs the approximation/band
margin argument; it is not free from the local power-count construction.

## Exact point where the general proof stops supplying geometry

Lemma 4.1 orders the exponents decreasingly and announces induction on s
(printed p. 37). For s=4 it gives the extremal profiles directly (p. 39).
After assuming the result for s-1, its general LOW case s.1 asserts

\[
(n-1,n-2,\ldots,n-s+2,n-s+1,n-s+1),
\]

where the decreasing prefix ends before the repeated last value. The
following page sums the powers to 2^n. The HIGH case s.2 asserts

\[
\kappa_1=n-1,\ldots,\quad
\kappa_{s-2}=\kappa_{s-1}=n-s+2,\quad\kappa_s=n-s+1,
\]

and sums the claimed powers to 2^n+2^(n-s+1), hence at most
2^n+2^(n-2). The arithmetic follows **once those extremal claims have
been justified**. The preceding text does not produce an actual legal
deformation, a permitted weighted center deletion, or a compatible
direction partition proving that a mixed/noSAT configuration is bounded
by these profiles. These are the unsupplied bridges; naming a profile
majorant or selector and assuming it would not repair them.

There is also an explicit range issue in using this display literally:
for s>n+1 the last exponent is negative, whereas every actual kappa_i is
a natural number and a primitive center is allowed. A valid argument
must provide a compression or a separate large-s case. Lemma 3.8 does
neither because it keeps s and all range-one centers.

Section 5 does not supply the omitted step elsewhere. Definition 5.1
calls a GC extreme when GA(V)=Ga(N)=a(N). Theorem 5.1 (pp. 44–46)
normalizes a minimizing sequence, takes a limit retaining pair-line
directions, and recursively resolves groups of points that collapse to
one limit point into smaller-scale circles. This establishes existence
of an extreme GC for fixed N. It does not state that an extreme GC has a
SAT center, a minimum number of range-one centers, or either exponent
profile from 4.1. If the limiting centers do not collapse, the proof
explicitly permits an ordinary extreme configuration. Existence alone
therefore cannot promote a mixed/noSAT actual model to the displayed
SAT-first profile.

The general clump lower bound retains the full original excess. To see
its force precisely, write Phi=4W+delta. Deleting one whole side leaves
p and the other side, hence at least three retained points. The geometric
window makes p gain at least one score, giving expense at least W; the
proper-subset budget gives side weight at least delta+W. In HO, the
large-angle B geometry forces the opposite B-side into one C-side
together with B. The other C-side costs at least delta+W, that B-side
costs at least delta+W, and B costs W. Thus mass outside C is at least
2delta+3W, while Phi-W=delta+3W, a contradiction since delta>0.

That last argument succeeds because its inclusions and disjoint sets
are proved for these two anchors. It cannot be extended by adding
different anchors' local clump bounds without such inclusions. Neither
the clump producer nor HO covers max kappa<=n-3, the absence of a wide
pair at a maximal vertex, or the absence of the required distinct second
nonacute maximal vertex. A noSAT hypothesis only bounds original scores
above by n-2; it does not itself furnish these HO inputs.

## Remaining producer boundary

For general LOW, the actual original Model and FixedCardMax interfaces
still need a new **proved geometric argument** handling the remaining
score/angle patterns and accounting for all original mass. The inspected
primary paper does not identify such an argument beyond asserting its
extremal profiles. The general HIGH assertions have the same missing
profile-comparison step; the LOW-specific clump and HO strip hypotheses
cannot be transported to HIGH. In particular, a global n-sector bound
cannot simply be asserted throughout HIGH, whose sharp construction
has capacity larger than 2^n.

No missing result is accepted here as a caller-provided selector, cover,
desired deletion inequality, or capacity statement. No disproven
maxGap statement is reinstated. No PAPER statement is introduced as a
Lean axiom. The useful positive output of this audit is the exact
local/global quantifier distinction in 3.3 versus 3.7, and the precise
unjustified transition in 4.1 s.1/s.2, not a new general closure claim.
